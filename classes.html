What students do

The STEAM Apprenticeship gives motivated Upper School students hands-on practice with advanced tools, design thinking, and Makerspace leadership.

Apprentices split their time between direct instruction, guided project work, and service that supports the day-to-day operation of the Makerspace.

  1. Makerspace Tools

    Operate tools and machines safely and confidently.

  2. Design Thinking Process

    Define, prototype, test, and refine ideas using design thinking.

  3. Student-led Workshops

    Lead peers through short, structured workshops.

  4. Engineering Journal

    Document work with technical drawings, photos, and reflections.

Key projects

Machine badges

Each major tool or machine has a three-tier badge path. Apprentices earn a badge by completing the assigned project and leading a workshop.

Student-led workshops

Every apprentice leads a 30-minute workshop on a skill or machine, from an intro to laser cutting or 3D printing to a design thinking crash course.

Passion projects

Level 2 apprentices design and build projects that balance advanced technical skills with creativity, form, and self-expression.

Community design challenges

Level 3 apprentices independently design and carry out human-centered challenges that serve the community and beyond.

How it works

Safety first

Apprentices pass the Makerspace safety quiz and sign a safety contract before beginning any work.

Document everything

Apprentices keep an Engineering Journal, updated at least once per cycle, with technical drawings, photos, and reflections.

Give back

Each apprentice has a service block that supports the day-to-day running of the Makerspace.

Grades reflect three things: skills shown through projects and workshops, documentation of the work, and professionalism, including attendance, engagement, and service to the community.

What students do

Makerspace: Design & Engineering is the intersection of all things art and technology. Students learn the tools and skills of the Makerspace and use them to create projects that blend art and technology.

Through observation, tinkering, trial and error, testing, and iterative design, students develop sketches and models, design in 2D and 3D, fabricate artifacts to test their designs, and learn code, logic, and computational thinking.

  1. Learn the tools and skills of the Makerspace
  2. Create projects that blend art and technology
  3. Use computational and design thinking to prototype solutions
  4. Use empathy to create meaningful designs
  5. Explore a variety of media and materials
  6. Design with purpose and practice creativity

Key projects

Final project

Students choose their own project and bring together multiple skills they have learned to create something spectacular. Sharing and explaining their ideas is part of the process.

Electric vehicle design challenge

Teams design, build, and test an electric vehicle against a set of design requirements.

How it works

Learning through doing

Teacher modeling introduces each skill and tool, and students then explore methods and materials on their own.

Engineering Journal

Students document every step of their process in a digital journal, which is reviewed once per cycle.

Pass/Fail with feedback

There is no homework. Rubrics and regular feedback encourage students to improve and iterate on their work.
